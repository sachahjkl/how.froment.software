## What was checked## What was checked, and what was not

- Checked: the link answers, and the page contains the words the text relies on (the four DORA measures,
  Conway's sentence, "blameless", "short-lived", "mainline", "interest", "motivated individuals").
- Not reached by a script: the CISA page on SBOM (it refuses automated requests), so it is not cited.
- Not checked: that Sigstore, SPDX, CycloneDX and SLSA do everything the text asks of the practice they illustrate.
  They are given as examples of the practice, not as the only way.
- Not checked in a source: that companies "spent days" finding out whether they ran Log4j. It is common knowledge, not a citation.
- Worked figures in the section on cost are arithmetic on example values (20 developers, 5 changes a day, 220 days), not measurements. The Scrum timeboxes are the Guide's maxima.
- Not checked: how the teams cited as examples organise their daily work in detail. They are cited for what they ship.
- Not established by any source: that agile causes these failures. The text argues that the default owns the results; no study isolates the cause. Wirth's complaint about slow software dates from 1995, before the manifesto.
- No comparable series exists for bug counts over the decades: the text gives speed, weight and resource figures, not a bug trend. "Few new uses" is the author's judgement.
- Deliberately not cited: Robertson, "Complex Systems Won't Survive the Competence Crisis": its core argument is political, not about software.
- Deliberately not cited: the "268% more likely to fail" figure, from a study commissioned by a consultancy selling another method.
- Not checked: that every reading the text gives of a source is the only fair one. A source is cited for what it
  says; the position built on it is the author's.
- The 90-day limit for dependencies, and the exact list of required measures, are the author's choices, not
  taken from a source.
